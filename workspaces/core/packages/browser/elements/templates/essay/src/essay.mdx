<Beat id="update">

Training moves a weight `w` to make a loss small. Take the loss `w²`: its slope is `2w`, and one step of gradient
descent moves the weight against the slope by a fraction of it, the **learning rate**.

At a learning rate of <Param name="rate" /> the weight after <Param name="steps" /> steps is <Value name="weight" />
(<FigureReference to="update-rule" />).

<Aside>
  Real networks have millions of weights, but each one moves by the same rule: its own slope times the rate.
</Aside>

<Beat id="rate">

Every step multiplies the weight by `1 − 2 × rate`, so the loss, which is the weight squared, shrinks by a factor
of <Value name="factor" /> per step. Press play to run ten steps.<Footnote id="quadratic">This is exact only for a
loss of `w²`. Other losses bend, but near a minimum they look the same.</Footnote>

<Predict

  answer="fast"
>

A rate of 0.4 multiplies the loss by 0.04 each step, a rate of 0.1 by 0.64. After ten steps one is about 10⁻¹⁴ and the
other about 10⁻². A rate of 0.5 lands on zero in one step.

</Predict>

<Beat id="blowup">

Nothing so far says a bigger rate is worse, so push it. The loss factor is `(1 − 2 × rate)²`. It stays below 1 for
every rate under 1, equals 1 at 1, and grows past it. At <Param name="rate" /> the factor is <Value name="factor" />
and the loss after <Value name="steps" /> steps is <Value name="loss" />.
